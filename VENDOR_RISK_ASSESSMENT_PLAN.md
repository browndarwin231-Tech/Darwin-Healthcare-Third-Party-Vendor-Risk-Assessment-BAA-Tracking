# Vendor Risk Assessment Plan — Nova Care Cloud (Simulated)

## Objectives
Document vendor scope, determine whether the vendor acts as a business associate based on actual functions and PHI access, collect relevant assurances, identify residual risk, and obtain a documented risk decision.

## Assessment steps
1. **Scope:** Record service, systems, data elements, subcontractors, and whether PHI/ePHI is created, received, maintained, or transmitted.
2. **Classify:** Assign inherent risk based on PHI sensitivity, service criticality, integration permissions, and operational dependence.
3. **Request evidence:** Relevant risk assessment, SOC 2 report or attestation where available, encryption safeguards, access review, incident response, recovery plan, and subcontractor list. A SOC 2 report is not a replacement for a BAA.
4. **Review BAA:** When the relationship requires a BAA, record execution date, version, restrictions, incident reporting, subcontractor terms, and data disposition obligations. Escalate missing or unsigned agreements before PHI sharing.
5. **Assess gaps:** Use illustrative likelihood (1–5) × impact (1–5). 1–5 Low; 6–11 Medium; 12–19 High; 20–25 Critical. These are internal training thresholds, not HIPAA-mandated scores.
6. **Decide:** Approve, conditionally approve with formal risk acceptance and remediation tracking, or block pending a prerequisite.
7. **Monitor:** Track expiry/review dates, evidence requests, exceptions, and material service changes.

## Escalations
- Missing executed BAA where required: **block PHI sharing** until legal/privacy review and execution.
- Risk score ≥ 20: CISO and privacy review before onboarding.
- Risk score ≥ 12 or missing key evidence: assign owner, due date, and formal decision.
- Vendor describing data as de-identified: validate actual data flows and method; do not assume a BAA is unnecessary based only on marketing language.

## Work product limitations
This project is a fictional case study using non-sensitive practice data. Actual HIPAA obligations depend on the parties' roles, contracts and data use.
